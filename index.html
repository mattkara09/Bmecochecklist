Two exams.
Eighty days.

Every day between now and the last paper has a named task for each subject. Nothing here says “revise Unit 3.” Open it in the morning, do the two blocks, tick them off.

Six rules that make this work

  1. Swap days, never skip them. Football, a SAC, a bad day — move the block to the next free slot. A missed day is a debt, not a write-off.
  2. SACs override everything. In the four days before a SAC, the SAC content replaces that subject's block.
  3. Never mark your own paper without the examiner report open. Score per question type, not per paper.
  4. Rewriting beats redoing. A rewritten answer at full marks teaches more than a fresh paper.
  5. Write, don't read. If a task doesn't produce ink on paper, it didn't happen.
  6. Stop at 8:30pm the night before an exam. Non-negotiable.

Where the material comes from

  • VCAA past examinations + examiner reports — vcaa.vic.edu.au. Economics and Business Management 2023, 2024, 2025 sit on the current (2023–2027) study design. Papers from 2016–2022 are still worth doing for technique; check each question against the current dot points before you trust the content.
  • The study designs themselves. Print the Unit 3 and Unit 4 dot points for both subjects and use them as a literal checklist. Every task below maps to one.
  • Commercial trial exams — VCTA Compak, Neap, Insight, TSSM, Kilbaha. Use these once you've exhausted VCAA papers, not before.
  • Economics data — ABS (CPI quarterly, Labour Force monthly, National Accounts, Balance of Payments), RBA (cash rate decisions, Statement on Monetary Policy), the 2026–27 Federal Budget papers.
  • Business Management case study — must be a real business, change within the last four years. Australian, listed or large private, with reported KPI movement. Verify every figure you quote.
